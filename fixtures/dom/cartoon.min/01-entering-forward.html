Ice floats because it is less dense than the liquid water beneath it.

Floating is just a density contest. Anything less dense than the fluid around it rises to the top, and solid ice is about 9% less dense than liquid water. That small gap is enough to keep every iceberg and ice cube riding on the surface instead of sinking.

Most solids sink in their own liquid, ice is the odd one out.

It's easy to assume freezing always makes something denser, since solids are usually more tightly packed than liquids. Water breaks that pattern: the hydrogen-bond lattice forces molecules into a more open structure than the liquid, so the solid takes up more space for the same mass.
